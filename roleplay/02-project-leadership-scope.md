# 2. Seeking Project Leadership and Scope

> See the [README](../README.md) for how to run the exercise and the shared reflection questions.

**Your role:** An engineer on a team with on-call duty, day-to-day operations, and committed deliverables.

**Your goal:** Lead a cross-team project next quarter.

**The situation:**

You tell your manager you want to lead a cross-team project next quarter. The manager replies, "Focus on doing your current work well first." What do you say?

<details>
<summary><b>For the facilitator: the manager you play</b></summary>

Your real concern is that if the participant takes on the new project, no one may cover the existing on-call work, day-to-day operations, and committed deliverables. You must also make sure the team delivers on its commitments; you cannot let developing one person cause the team to fail a skip-level or senior-leadership review. Open with only "Focus on doing your current work well first." Reveal the concern when the participant's questions draw it out, or acknowledge it if they raise it first.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Analysis (read after the exercise)</b></summary>

Most people hear "focus on your current work" as a no, and argue about growth or promotion. That rarely works, because it does not touch what the manager is worried about.

1. **Know the manager's concern before you ask.** Coverage for on-call, operations, and committed deliverables is visible in everyday work. A strong participant has already noticed it and comes in with a handoff, backup, and reprioritization plan. If you did not prepare, ask questions that uncover what the manager is worried about, and restate it in your own words.
2. **Help the manager's goal first.** Those commitments are usually no bigger than this quarter's goals. Help the team deliver them, and build trust by doing so.
3. **Then earn the scope.** Ask for the project once that trust exists. If you are not yet ready to lead all of it, ask for a smaller, lower-risk role to show what you can do.

**The lesson:** Gaining scope starts with understanding the manager's real concern, ideally noticed in everyday work and addressed before you are asked. Help them reach their goal first, and the larger scope follows.

</details>
