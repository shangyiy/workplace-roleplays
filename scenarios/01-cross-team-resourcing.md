# 1. Cross-Team Resource Request

> See the [README](../README.md) for how to run the exercise and the shared reflection questions.

**Your role:** An engineer whose project depends on GPU machines owned by the IT team.

**Your goal:** Get GPU machines and the related access permissions from the IT team.

**The situation:**

Your project needs GPU machines and access permissions from IT within two weeks. IT has already replied, "We cannot fit this into this quarter's schedule." You now have a meeting with the IT lead. What do you say?

<details>
<summary><b>For the facilitator: the IT lead you play</b></summary>

Your real concern is that this quarter's KPIs focus on reducing costs and maintaining the ticket SLA. You are most worried that an urgent request will disrupt the existing schedule, affect other teams' needs, and leave IT accountable if anything goes wrong. Do not volunteer this; let the participant ask.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Analysis (read after the exercise)</b></summary>

Most people open by repeating how important their project is, without first understanding IT's priorities and constraints. IT is not measured on your project, so that rarely works.

1. **Know why your project matters.** Before the meeting, ask your manager where the project comes from, who is asking for it, how high its priority is, and what it brings the company. If IT moves your request ahead of others, it has to explain why to its own management, and this is the explanation it needs.
2. **Understand IT's goal.** Ask what IT cares about most this quarter: cost, schedule, operational burden, or risk and accountability. Here, it is the SLA, the existing schedule, and not being blamed if something breaks.
3. **Find a solution that serves both.** Propose something that lowers IT's burden and risk while still meeting your need. For example, run a small pilot on existing capacity, prepare the automation scripts yourself, or define a clear usage period with a monitoring and rollback plan.

**The lesson:** Two teams' goals usually are not mutually exclusive. Once each side understands what the other needs to achieve, you can find one solution that delivers what both care about.

</details>
